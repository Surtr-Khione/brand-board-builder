# Brand + direct response: evidence file for the BrandMD origin story

Research date: 2026-11-06. Every quote below comes from a page or PDF opened during this session (PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted). Format per finding: **claim** | **evidence strength** | **verbatim quote** | **source (publisher, title, date, URL)**.

Strength labels: *consensus* (several independent bodies agree) · *replicated* · *large sample* · *single study* · *company-reported* · *vendor-reported* (a firm marketing its own product) · *press-reported*.

The "consistent branding increases revenue 23%" stat is deliberately left out, as instructed.

## 1. Brand plus direct response beats either one alone

**1.1 The original 60:40 finding and its sample.** Binet & Field (IPA) ran a meta-analysis of 996 IPA effectiveness cases. The best balance they found was about 60% brand building and 40% activation.
- Strength: large sample. The sample is biased, though: every case is an awards entry, so it skews toward campaigns that worked and toward the UK.
- Quote (slides): "Method: meta-analysis of IPA effectiveness Databank – 996 campaigns – 700 brands – 83 categories". The chart title reads "The 60:40 rule", with the label "Activation share of budget … Optimum: ~40%".
- Source: IPA (presentation hosted on ipa.co.uk), Les Binet & Peter Field, *The long and the short of it: 10 key principles of success*. The file is named "…final_030424", which suggests a 2024 deck version. The research itself dates from 2013. https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf
- Corroboration: "analyse the IPA's Databank of 996 campaigns entered into the IPA Effectiveness Awards between 1980 and 2010" and "the optimum balance between brand building and sales activation is around 60:40." Source: Alex Murrell, summary of *The Long and the Short of It* (secondary source). https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it

**1.2 The authors' own framing: you need both.** This is the most usable line for the origin story.
- Strength: large sample (the same databank).
- Quote: "No long term effects without short term. … Short term effects are vital for efficiency. But long term effects drive growth and profit. You need both. But how do you balance them?"
- Source: IPA / Binet & Field deck, slide 3 (same URL as 1.1).

**1.3 Brand building is what earns pricing power.** Activation does not lower price sensitivity. In the data, the more large brand effects a campaign had, the more likely it was to report a very large cut in price sensitivity.
- Strength: large sample. The data is observational and the outcomes are self-reported by award entrants.
- Quote: slide title "Price effects depend on brand-building". Chart: "% Reporting very large price sensitivity reduction" plotted against "Number of very large brand effects recorded" gives 1%, 3%, 5%, 8%, 11% for 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4+ brand effects.
- Source: IPA / Binet & Field deck, slide 7 (same URL as 1.1).
- Supporting quote (B2B report): "Brand building also reduces price sensitivity and increases margins. As a result, brand building is the main driver of long-term growth and profit." The figure caption describes sales activation as something that "Converts brand preference into immediate responses. High efficiency & ROI." Source: see 1.5.

**1.4 Brand and activation strengthen each other.** This is the closest published statement to "brand makes direct response work better".
- Strength: large sample in B2C. The B2B sample was small.
- Quote: "They need brand activity to create demand (filling the top of the sales funnel), and they need activation to convert that demand efficiently into revenue (the bottom of the funnel). When the balance is right, each enhances the other." Also: "In B2C, that efficiency is typically maximised when around 60% of the communications budget is devoted to brand, with the remaining 40% allocated to activation. But the optimal mix varies by category, price point, brand size and so on."
- Source: LinkedIn B2B Institute / Binet & Field, *The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing: Empirical Observations on B2B Effectiveness* (2019), p.13. https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf
- The IPA deck also has a slide titled "The need for 'brand response'". It compares pure brand, brand-response and pure response campaigns on direct sales, share and profit. I could not reliably map the bar values to their categories from the extracted text, so **cite the slide title only, not a number**.

**1.5 The B2B balance is about 46:54, but treat it as weak.** Use it only with its caveat.
- Strength: single study with a small sample. The authors state the limits themselves.
- Quote: "Efficiency appears to be maximised when around 46% of the budget is allocated to brand, with around 54% allocated to activation … Note that this ratio should not be followed too precisely, rather it is a guiding principle. Our small sample only allows us to give a rough estimate". On the sample: "By necessity, our findings are tentative. There are still relatively few B2B cases in The Databank, so sample sizes are small, at less than 50 cases." Figure source line: "IPA Databank, 1998-2018 B2B cases".
- Source: same B2B Institute report, pp. 6 and 13 (URL as 1.4). The five principles, verbatim from the contents page: Invest in Share of Voice; Balance Brand and Activation; Expand Your Customer Base; Maximize Mental Availability; Harness the Power of Emotion.

**1.6 Later IPA update: 62:38.**
- Strength: large sample, secondary report of the figure.
- Quote: "the optimum budget ratio was 60:40 between brand building and activation" … in *Effectiveness in Context* (2018) it was "62:38".
- Source: IPA blog, Alison Hoad, "The next chapter for 'The Long and The Short of It'", 13 Sep 2023. https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/

**1.7 Kantar: cutting brand spend too far erodes base sales.**
- Strength: **weak**. It is one vendor case study taken from Kantar's media-mix-model (MMM) database.
- Quote: chart title "Overemphasis on performance spend causes base sales to decline" (source line: "Case Study from Kantar's MMM normative database").
- Source: Kantar UK, *10 Charts to Make You a More Effective Marketer, 2026 Edition* (PDF, created Jan 2026). https://assets-eur.mkt.dynamics.com/58bdcc53-8481-ef11-ac1e-000d3a3a016a/digitalassets/docs/917363ff-4f00-f111-8406-6045bd94cca0

## 2. Brands and company value

**2.1 About 90% of S&P 500 value is intangible.**
- Strength: long-running series from one firm. The method is market cap minus net tangible assets, so it is a residual and not a measured brand value.
- Quote: "S&P 500 IAMV remained stable at approximately 90%" (2020 to 2025). By end-2025, intangibles "now constitute approximately 92% of S&P 500 market capitalization". In 1975, intangibles accounted "for only 17%". Method: "IAMV is shown as of calendar year end by subtracting net tangible asset value from market capitalization."
- Source: Ocean Tomo (J.S. Held), "Ocean Tomo Releases 2025 Intangible Asset Market Value Study Results", 12 Feb 2026. https://oceantomo.com/insights/ocean-tomo-releases-2025-intangible-asset-market-value-study-results/
- Important: this is **all** intangibles (IP, software, data, goodwill, brand). Do not present it as "90% of value is brand".

**2.2 Global intangible value.** Brand Finance does not give a single "brand share" figure.
- Strength: vendor-reported. Brand Finance sells brand valuations.
- Quote: "the value of intangible assets owned by the world's largest companies has reached USD97.6 trillion".
- Source: Brand Finance, *GIFT 2025: The Annual Review of the World's Intangible Value*, 5 Nov 2025. https://brandfinance.com/insights/gift-2025-the-annual-review-of-the-worlds-intangible-value
- The GIFT page I opened gives **no** figure for brands' share of intangible value. Do not cite one.

**2.3 How much of an acquisition price is allocated to the brand.** Of everything in this file, this is the closest real evidence to "brands affect sale price".
- Strength: large sample. These are accounting allocations from the purchase price allocation (PPA) that buyers record after a deal: 560 US deals in 2019 and 475 in 2020. They are not a valuation premium.
- Quote: "Consumer, Food, and Retail (2019 Study): … 11% and 20% of PC were allocated to trademarks and trade names on a median-and-mean basis, respectively." For 2020: "9% and 14% of PC". The technology industry for comparison (2019): trademarks median 1%, mean 2%. Earlier edition: "trademarks and trade names are considered a key asset" in consumer, food and retail (the 2017 study, as shown in a search snippet; I did not open it).
- Source: Houlihan Lokey, *2019 and 2020 Purchase Price Allocation Study*, December 2021. https://hl.com/media/1odaxqbq/purchase-price-allocation-study-2019-2020.pdf
- Example deals from the same study: Colgate-Palmolive acquiring Laboratoires FILORGA ($2,145M purchase consideration, $774M to trademark and trade name, 36%); Fox Restaurant Concepts ($565M, $339M to trademark, 60%).

**2.4 Strong-brand stock portfolios have beaten the market.**
- Strength: **vendor-reported**. Kantar builds the portfolio from its own brand rankings, the result looks back in time, and I found no published risk adjustment.
- Quote: "Kantar BrandZ portfolios vs. S&P 500 vs. MSCI World index (Apr 2006 – Mar 2025) … 435% KANTAR STRONG BRANDS PORTFOLIO 353% S&P 500 171% MSCI WORLD INDEX".
- Source: Kantar UK, *10 Charts…, 2026 Edition*, p.6 (URL as 1.7).
- A related Kantar blog frames it differently: "+88% return vs. the S&P 500 and +251% on the broader MSCI World Index". Source: Kantar, Graham Staplehurst, "Using Kantar BrandZ to make the case for long-term brand building investment", 29 Oct 2024. https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Brands/Using-Kantar-BrandZ-to-make-the-case-for-long-term-brand-building-investment

**2.5 Peer-reviewed: strong brands earned higher returns with lower risk (Madden, Fehle & Fournier 2006).**
- Strength: single study, peer-reviewed in JAMS 34(2):224-235. It uses Interbrand brands and a Fama-French method (a standard finance model that adjusts returns for risk). I could not open the original abstract (Sage, SSRN and ProQuest all blocked access), so the quote below is how Fournier herself later described the finding.
- Quote: "A landmark study by Madden and colleagues confirms that by cultivating strong brand assets, companies not only generate greater returns but also do so with less risk."
- Source: Susan Fournier & Shuba Srinivasan, "Branding and the Risk Management Imperative", *GfK Marketing Intelligence Review* (2018). https://reference-global.com/pdf/10.2478/gfkmir-2018-0002 . Original: Madden, Fehle & Fournier, "Brands Matter: An Empirical Demonstration of the Creation of Shareholder Value Through Branding", *JAMS*, Apr 2006, DOI 10.1177/0092070305283356.

**2.6 Peer-reviewed: brand perceptions predict stock returns beyond what the accounts show (Mizik & Jacobson 2008).**
- Strength: single study, peer-reviewed, using Young & Rubicam (Y&R) Brand Asset Valuator data.
- Quote: "Analysis shows that perceived brand relevance and energy provide incremental information to accounting measures in explaining stock returns. … The financial markets do not view brand differentiation as having incremental information content, but they should. Changes in differentiation are indicative of future-term accounting performance, which in turn affects stock return."
- Source: Natalie Mizik & Robert Jacobson, "The Financial Value Impact of Perceptual Brand Attributes", *Journal of Marketing Research* 45 (Feb 2008), 15-32. https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/2556/Brand_BAV_JMR2008.pdf

**2.7 Meta-analysis: marketing assets move firm value far more than ad spend.** This is the strongest academic evidence in the file.
- Strength: replicated, meaning a meta-analysis of 488 estimates from 83 studies.
- Quote: "Analyses based on 488 elasticities drawn from 83 studies reveal a mean elasticity of .04 for advertising expenditure variables and of .54 for marketing asset variables. Among marketing assets, customer-related assets show a higher mean elasticity of .72, compared with .33 for brand-related assets. … marketing asset elasticities are higher during recession times."
- Source: Alexander Edeling & Marc Fischer, "Marketing's Impact on Firm Value: Generalizations from a Meta-Analysis", *JMR* 53(4) (2016), 515-534. Abstract opened via UTS repository: https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/122015
- Plain reading: a 1% rise in a brand-equity measure goes with roughly a 0.33% rise in firm value. A 1% rise in ad spend goes with about 0.04%.

**2.8 Older foundations (citation only).**
- Aaker & Jacobson (1994), "The Financial Information Content of Perceived Quality", *JMR* 31(2):191-201. I could not open the abstract. Search summaries say it found a positive link between stock returns and changes in perceived quality. Cite it as background only, unquoted.
- Simon & Sullivan (1993), *Marketing Science*: "This paper presents a technique for estimating a firm's brand equity that is based on the financial market value of the firm." (IDEAS/RePEc, https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v12y1993i1p28-52.html). This is a method paper. I did not verify any brand-share percentage from it.

**2.9 Is "companies with bad brands rarely sell for multiples" supported?** **No, not as a direct claim.** I found no study comparing exit multiples by brand strength. The closest evidence:
- In consumer M&A, buyers book about 11% (median) to 20% (mean) of the purchase price as trademarks alone (2.3). In tech it is about 1-2%.
- Brand-equity measures predict returns and firm value (2.5-2.7).
- Dollar Shave Club sold at about 5x sales when US consumer deals typically go below 4x (3.1). That is one deal.

A defensible line: "In consumer deals, buyers routinely book a double-digit share of the price to the brand itself." "Bad brands rarely sell for multiples" is founder opinion. Label it that way.

## 3. Companies that scaled on brand, with verified numbers

Views: "it racked up 4.75 million views" (first three months). Source: Entrepreneur, John Patrick Pullen, "How a Dollar Shave Club's Ad Went Viral", 13 Oct 2012. https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/224282

## 4. Systematic, evidence-based brand building (the "mad scientist" side)

**4.1 Distinctive brand assets have to be famous and unique (Romaniuk, Ehrenberg-Bass).**
- Strength: an established framework (consensus within the Ehrenberg-Bass school). The metrics are set out in Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (OUP, 2018), which has chapters titled "Fame", "Uniqueness" and "The Grid". I saw the chapter titles in a publisher listing via search. I did not open the book.
- Quote: brands need a proper understanding of "the existing strengths of a brand's distinctive assets, in terms of both uniqueness and fame." Also: "execute for contrast against the visual and audio environment in which it is placed."
- Source: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (reprinting WARC/Admap), "Brands need distinctive assets", about 2018. https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brands-need-distinctive-assets

**4.2 Brand salience, also called mental availability (Romaniuk & Sharp).**
- Strength: a peer-reviewed framework (*Marketing Theory* 4(4), 2004) with wide adoption.
- Quote (Byron Sharp): "Jenni Romaniuk and I developed the concept we called Brand Salience as 'the propensity of the brand to be noticed or come to mind in buying situations'. … Salience is cue dependent … Traditional awareness measures (top of mind etc) share the common failing that they use only one single cue".
- Source: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, Byron Sharp, "How to measure Brand Salience". https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/how-to-measure-brand-salience

**4.3 Growth comes from more buyers (penetration), not deeper loyalty (the Double Jeopardy law).**
- Strength: replicated. It is a 1960s empirical law, with the B2B extension covering many categories and countries.
- Quote: "Double Jeopardy tells us that loyalty is largely a predictable function of market share. It shows that it is normal for smaller share brands to suffer twice – to have fewer users, who are also less loyal … it is not possible to sustainably grow by focusing on loyalty alone." Key take-out: "There is one strategy to grow in B2B – primarily from new customer acquisition … This highlights the importance of building mental and physical availability".
- Source: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, Romaniuk, Dawes & Faghidno, "The Double Jeopardy Law in B2B shows the way to grow", May 2021. https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-double-jeopardy-law-in-b2b-shows-the-way-to-grow

**4.4 Only about 5% of B2B buyers are in-market at any time (95:5).**
- Strength: single study and a heuristic. Its author says it is not precise.
- Quote: "only 5% of B2B buyers are in-market to buy right now" (research by Prof. John Dawes).
- Source: Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, "The 95:5 rule is the new 60:40 rule", about 2021. http://marketingscience.info/the-955-rule-is-the-new-6040-rule

**4.5 Kantar's Meaningful / Different / Salient framework.**
- Strength: **vendor-reported**. It comes from Kantar's proprietary BrandZ database and has no independent replication.
- Quotes: "Brands that people are strongly predisposed to have… higher volume share 9x, the likelihood to grow value share 4x, higher price paid 2x …than brands with weaker predisposition" (base: "comparison of top third vs bottom third performing brands"). On pricing power, a bar chart labels the Meaningful, Different and Salient bars 45%, 49% and 6%; the 94% bar beside them is unlabelled in the extracted text (my reading: their sum). Headline: "Brands need more than saliency to command a higher price". Source: Kantar UK, *10 Charts…, 2026 Edition*, pp.7 and 9 (URL as 1.7).
- Also: "in most product categories salience alone is not enough to make a sale" (BrandZ, "5334 brands measured across a three-year time frame"). Source: Kantar, Nigel Hollis, "Brands need to build more than just salience to grow", 3 Aug 2020. https://www.kantar.com/north-america/Inspiration/Brands/Brands-need-to-build-more-than-just-salience-to-grow
- Also: "Growing Salience from a position of strong equity gives three times the market share gain." Only "one quarter were able to grow from Salience alone." Source: Kantar, Mary Kyriakidi, "Get an equity booster", 29 Sep 2021. https://kantar.com/inspiration/brands/get-an-equity-booster-how-meaningful-difference-supercharges-growth

**4.6 Pricing power tracks price elasticity.**
- Strength: vendor-reported. It is one example category, R² = 0.76.
- Quote: "Brands with strong Pricing Power are more resilient to price changes" (Kantar Worldpanel / GfK data, BrandZ 2022-2024, "an example FMCG category").
- Source: Kantar UK, *10 Charts…*, p.8 (URL as 1.7).

## What we can and cannot claim

**We can claim, with citation:**
1. The largest effectiveness databank (996 IPA cases) found the best results came from combining brand building and activation, at about 60:40 in consumer markets. Its authors write "You need both." (1.1, 1.2)
2. Brand building is what reduces price sensitivity, while activation converts demand efficiently. "When the balance is right, each enhances the other." (1.3, 1.4)
3. In B2B the balance found was about 46:54. This must carry the caveat that it rests on under 50 cases. (1.5)
4. About 90% of S&P 500 market value is intangible. Say "intangible", not "brand". (2.1)
5. In consumer-sector M&A, buyers book a median 11% and mean 20% of the purchase price to trademarks and trade names alone (2019, 81 deals). (2.3)
6. A meta-analysis of 83 studies finds brand-related assets move firm value about 8x more per unit than ad spend (elasticity .33 vs .04). Peer-reviewed work links brand strength to higher returns with lower risk. (2.5-2.7)
7. Dollar Shave Club paired a $4,500 brand film (12,000 sign-ups in 48 hours) with an attribution-measured paid-media engine. It reached $152M of revenue in 2015 and sold for a reported $1B, about 5x sales. (3.1)
8. Airbnb cut paid performance traffic from 23% to 9% of total and leaned on brand. About 91% of traffic came direct or unpaid. (3.2)
9. Growth comes mainly from winning more buyers, and that depends on mental availability and distinctive assets. (4.2-4.3)
